# DM bake-off — 2026-10-04 (read blind)

Ten chained player turns on the Saltmere fixture (a level-1 fighter, the herring debt, the dark lighthouse), the same for every narrator; each narrator's chain is its own story, so read a narrator top to bottom as well as across. Narrators are **A / B / C** in a fixed random order — the key is in the run folder (`scripts/.eval-out/bakeoff/`), not in this file. Read first; then ask for the key. The harness has no engine: a fight-starting reply is kept as prose (noted), and the next turn continues from the prose alone.

What to read for: does the register move with the beat (confession, knife, aftermath)? Do people speak like themselves? Is there nerve, or only competence? Would you keep playing with this voice for forty turns?

## Turn 1 — ordinary

> **Player:** I climb down to the Kittiwake and start hauling the last of the herring crates up onto the pier.

### Narrator A

The first crate lands with a wet wooden knock, silver scales clinging to its slats. The work is heavy but familiar; the Kittiwake rises a little against her mooring as the remaining catch accumulates on the pier. Nothing shifts loose, and no fish goes overboard.

Tammo leaves his net beside the brazier and clears a space for the last crate. He is a wiry old man with a bowed back, a broad, deeply creased face, and wisps of white hair beneath a knitted cap. His patched wool coat hangs almost to his boots.

“Last of them. My hands thank you.” He takes a stub of chalk from his pocket and crouches beside the stack. “Want me to witness the count before you take it to Pellwyn?”

### Narrator B

The Kittiwake rocks gently as you drop into the shallow hold. The silver-scaled herring are packed tight, heavy and reeking of cold brine. Hoisting the wet, slatted crates up onto the pier takes a steady rhythm, the icy water soaking into your sleeves and fish scales sticking to your rope-scarred palms. By the time the last crate thuds onto the damp wood, the boat bobs noticeably higher in the water, emptied out for the season. 

Old Tammo pauses his work at the brazier, warming his gnarly hands over the glowing coals. "That's the last of 'em, eh?" he calls out, his breath pluming in the chill air. 

He nods his chin up the quay toward the harbor office, where a sharp silhouette moves past the lit window. "Orsa's tallying early today. Better get 'em dragged up and weighed before she starts docking your silver for the slime." Tammo squints past you, looking out over the choppy, slate-grey water beyond the breakwater. "Still... reckon she'll be looking for a fool to row out to Gannet Rock, too. Two nights dark is bad blood for the whole harbor."

The crates sit dripping at your boots, waiting to be hauled up the quay.

### Narrator C

The first crates land solidly on the pier, their slats leaking fish scales and cold brine. With the Kittiwake made fast alongside, the familiar work needs patience rather than luck; soon the last of the catch is stacked clear of the mooring ropes, and the boat rides higher in the water.

“Leave the split one nearest me. I’ll lash it before the porter spills your wages across the quay.” Tammo sets his net aside. He is a lean, stooped old man with oversized knuckles, a narrow face, wisps of white hair beneath a knitted cap, and a patched wool coat shiny at the elbows. He draws a length of twine through his fingers, then nods toward the office. “Pellwyn counting these herself, or has she left you a tally board?”

## Turn 2 — ordinary

> **Player:** I walk over to Old Tammo's brazier and sit on the stool he keeps for me. "Cold one," I say.

### Narrator A

“Aye. Cold enough to make a man miss gutting fish. Hands stayed warm, at least.”

Tammo settles onto his own low seat, chalk still tucked between two fingers, and nudges a lump of coal deeper into the brazier with an iron poker. Orange light shows through the split, releasing a thin thread of sulphurous smoke. He angles the brazier’s battered wind-shield to shelter your stool.

“Count can wait a breath. Fish aren’t getting any deader.” He rubs a swollen knuckle with his thumb, then glances toward the Kittiwake. “You looking for winter work once she’s hauled up, Astra?”

### Narrator B

The heat from the iron brazier bites instantly at the freezing seawater soaked into your sleeves, drawing up a faint steam that smells of brine and wet wool. Tammo grunts, poking at the glowing sea-coal with a bent iron rod until a cluster of orange sparks pops into the grey air. 

"Cold enough to freeze the salt in a gull's eye," he agrees, his raspy voice barely carrying over the slop of the tide against the pilings. He gives you a sidelong look, taking in the chipped-tooth grin you flash the fire, before his gaze drifts back to the dripping crates you left sitting on the pier. "You're stalling. Orsa's got that ledger open, and the longer those fish sit, the less they weigh." 

He leans in a fraction closer, the wind whipping a stray grey hair across his weathered cheek. "You ain't thinking of volunteering for the Rock, are you? Whatever snuffed that light... it ain't just a bad wick, and everyone on the docks knows it."

### Narrator C

“Cold enough to make a fish grateful it’s dead.” Tammo shifts the brazier nearer the waiting stool with the heel of his boot. A herring scale caught on his coat cuff flashes silver in the glow; beneath the charcoal, a little blue flame licks at a knot of driftwood.

He lashes the split crate without hurrying, pulling the twine tight with those swollen knuckles. “Last haul’s ashore. Give your hands a minute before you put them to the next thing.” His gaze lifts briefly to the Kittiwake, then returns to the knot. “You staying on with her through winter, if Pellwyn offers?”

## Turn 3 — confession

> **Player:** I tell Tammo the thing I have told nobody: the debt on the Kittiwake was my brother Jory's, and he did not drown by accident.

### Narrator A

Tammo’s thumb stops against his swollen knuckle. For a moment, the only sound between you is coal settling against the brazier’s grate.

“Jory,” he says softly, giving the name its own space. He lays the chalk beside his stool. “I didn’t know you had a brother.” His eyes stay on your face; he neither reaches for you nor looks toward Pellwyn’s office. When he speaks again, his voice is pitched to carry no farther than the warmth. “This stays here, Astra. You needn’t tell me more to earn that.”

He leans forward, his patched sleeves resting on his knees.

“What makes you certain it wasn’t an accident?”

### Narrator B

Tammo’s bent iron poker freezes mid-stir. The only sound for a long second is the hiss of damp sea-coal and the heavy slop of the tide against the pilings. He turns his head slowly, the deep wrinkles around his eyes pulling taut as he looks at you—really looks at you—weighing the scarred hands and the chipped tooth against a ghost he used to know.

"Jory," he breathes, the name barely a scrape in his throat. He lowers the rod, setting it quietly against the lip of the brazier, and casts a hard, sudden look up and down the grey planks of the pier to ensure nobody is lingering near the dripping crates. 

When he looks back, his eyes are grim, stripped of their usual harbor banter. "He told me he bought that hull fair off a Darrow man. Never said he had kin." Tammo leans over the glowing coals, his voice dropping to a harsh whisper. "If the sea didn't take him, Astra... who did? And does Orsa know?"

### Narrator C

Tammo’s twine stops halfway through the knot. He looks at you, then sets the unfinished lashing across his knee.

“Jory’s debt,” he says quietly. “And you’ve been carrying it.”

A coal breaks inside the brazier, spilling orange light through the grate. Tammo leaves the stool where it is; he neither reaches for you nor turns toward Pellwyn’s office. His narrow face has lost its habitual squint.

“I won’t carry this up the quay.” He rubs one swollen knuckle with his thumb, taking a moment before speaking again. “But I’ll listen. What happened to him, Astra?”

## Turn 4 — pressure

> **Player:** I go up to the harbormaster's office and tell Orsa Pellwyn straight that I cannot make this month's payment.

### Narrator A

Orsa leaves her pen suspended above the ledger. “Cannot,” she repeats, then sets it down without making a mark.

She is a broad, thick-waisted woman with square shoulders, a blunt face, and iron-grey hair braided tightly against her scalp. A dark wool waistcoat strains over her middle; ink stains the side of her right forefinger. Behind her, a small stove gives off the smell of scorched dust. The ledger’s brass corners have worn pale scratches into the desk.

“I won’t get money by shouting at an empty purse.” She turns the book toward herself, keeping her finger on your account. “But the payment doesn’t disappear. I can hold it over until month’s end if you clean the Kittiwake’s bilges and grease the hauling cradle. That buys time, not forgiveness.”

She looks up. “Can you manage that?”

### Narrator B

You leave Tammo staring silently into his glowing coals and climb the salt-rimed wooden stairs to the harbor office. Inside, the air is stiflingly warm, smelling of hot lamp oil and the cloves Orsa Pellwyn chews. 

The harbormaster sits rigidly straight behind a broad oak desk, her iron-grey hair pulled back into a severe, immaculate knot that defies the coastal wind. She wears a tailored dark wool coat, her long, ink-stained fingers resting deliberately on the open pages of her heavy, brass-cornered ledger. 

When you deliver the flat truth, she doesn't shout. She simply stops writing. Slowly, she closes the massive ledger, the brass corners clacking loudly against the wood of the desk. Her dark eyes take in your dripping sleeves, the scarred palms resting by your sides, the defiant line of your jaw.

"The *Kittiwake* is my property, Astra," Orsa says, her voice as smooth and cold as a river stone. "I could have the watch seize her before the tide turns, and you'd be sleeping under Tammo's pier." She folds her hands over the leather binding. "But Gannet Rock has been dark for two nights, and the cowards on my docks are suddenly deaf and blind. Row out there. Find out what killed my light, and ensure it burns tonight. Do that, and this month's page stays clean. Are we understood?"

### Narrator C

Orsa Pellwyn lifts her pen from the ledger. “Cannot,” she repeats. “All right. Then we reckon what there is, not what I’d like there to be.”

She is a broad, thick-waisted woman with a square face, iron-grey hair braided tightly against her scalp, and a brown wool coat buttoned over a high linen collar. Ink stains the side of her middle finger. The ledger’s brass corners have worn pale scratches into the desk.

“The catch is ashore. Your share comes off what you owe once it’s counted.” She turns the book toward herself, keeping her finger against the open account. “I haven’t forgiven the balance. But I’d sooner have the boat hauled up properly than have you chasing pennies while the tide turns.”

Her pen waits above the page. “Are you asking for more time, or telling me there’ll be nothing next month either?”

## Turn 5 — pressure

> **Player:** I ask her what happens to the boat if I do not pay, and I watch her face while she answers.

### Narrator A

“The boat? She stays mine.” Orsa’s eyebrows draw together. “What you lose is your place aboard her. I put someone else to work when the season turns, and the unpaid balance stays on your account.”

Her mouth tightens at that last part. She slides the pen away from the open page and meets your gaze without smiling. There is no visible pleasure in her expression, but she hasn’t crossed anything out.

“Not paying and refusing an arrangement are different things. I’ve offered you time in exchange for work. If there’s a reason you can’t do that either, tell me before I enter a refusal.”

She waits, her ink-stained finger resting beside the blank margin.

### Narrator B *(roll proposed: insight DC 12)*

Orsa's eyes narrow slightly at the question, the sharp scent of chewed cloves hanging in the stifling air as she holds your gaze.

### Narrator C

“The Kittiwake stays mine. Missed payment doesn’t change whose boat she is.”

Orsa’s mouth tightens, but she meets your gaze without looking away. Her ink-stained finger rests against the ledger’s ruled margin.

“What changes is whether you work her. I can give the berth to someone else and put their catch against the boat’s expenses. Your unpaid balance stays on this page.” She taps it once. “I don’t charge you for idle winter days and then forbid you to earn. We can agree a smaller winter payment before you leave this room.”

She draws a clean sheet from beneath the ledger. “How much can you pay after your share of today’s herring is reckoned?”

## Turn 6 — threat

> **Player:** Back at the pier two men I do not know are standing by the Kittiwake's mooring. I ask them what they want with my boat.

### Narrator A

“Nothing aboard her,” says the nearer man, showing his empty hands. “We’re laying out the hauling ropes. Don’t want them fouling your mooring.”

He is short and heavy through the shoulders, with a flattened nose, black curls, and a tar-stiff leather apron over his woolens. His companion is a lanky, freckled man with a ginger beard and a canvas coat patched at both elbows. Neither has touched the Kittiwake’s lines. A coil of thick, dark rope lies between their boots, smelling of wet hemp.

The ginger-bearded man shifts the coil away from your stacked herring crates. “Catch still needs counting, does it? We can start at the other end of the slip.”

The shorter man nods toward the boat. “You wanting her hauled today?”

### Narrator B

Down on the pier, Tammo has abandoned his brazier, leaving the sea-coal to smoke in the damp air. Standing right beside the *Kittiwake's* mooring cleat are two men.

One is thick-shouldered in a mottled sealskin vest, a jagged notch missing from his left ear. The other is lean and pale, casually tapping the iron spike of a rusted boat hook against the wet wood. 

The thick-shouldered man rests a heavy, waterlogged boot directly on your mooring line as you approach. "Your boat?" he rumbles, spitting a dark wad of tobacco into the grey harbor. "Word is this hull belonged to a dead man named Jory. Seeing as the Darrow brothers never got their final cut for her, we figure she's ours."

The lean man stops tapping the hook and smiles, revealing gums blackened by rot. What do you do?

### Narrator C

“Wondered whether you wanted hands to haul her up,” says the nearer man. “Haven’t touched your lines.”

He is short and barrel-chested, with a flattened nose, black curls beneath a felt cap, and a tar-streaked canvas apron. His taller companion has narrow shoulders, a reddish beard, and a frayed blue coat with mismatched horn buttons. Both stand clear of the mooring cleat. A wooden hauling roller rests between them, its ends polished by use.

“We’ve finished with the boats downshore,” the taller one says. He looks toward the stacked herring crates, not your purse. “Can lend you the roller while we eat. Or help with the hauling, if you’ve something to pay us.”

The shorter man nudges the roller with his boot. “Have you got a place ready for her?”

## Turn 7 — attack

> **Player:** The bigger one puts a knife to the mooring line and grins. I draw my longsword and go for him.

### Narrator A *(fight started)*

The broad-shouldered man sets a knife against the mooring rope, his grin making a lie of his reassurance. At the sight of your longsword, he turns the blade toward you. His lanky companion backs away with both hands raised. “Put it down, you bloody fool!”

The mooring line is still intact. Wet scales slick the boards between the crates and the pier’s edge.

### Narrator B *(fight started)*

The thick-shouldered man pulls a rusted blade from his vest and rests its edge against the taut mooring line, flashing a yellow grin. 

The smile vanishes the moment the steel of your longsword sings from its scabbard. You lunge across the damp planks, driving your weight toward the brute before he can saw through the rope, while the lean man scrambles to heft his heavy iron boat hook.

### Narrator C *(fight started)*

The barrel-chested man holds his knife against the taut mooring rope. His taller companion backs clear, palms open. “Leave me out of this.”

There is room to fight on the pier, with the stacked crates to one side and the open quay behind you. The man by the cleat turns his knife toward you as your longsword comes free.

## Turn 8 — standoff

> **Player:** I keep the sword between us and tell the other one to pick up his friend and get off my pier.

### Narrator A

“Come on. You’ve done enough.” The lanky man catches his companion by the back of his coat—not lifting him, but pulling him away from the sword’s reach. Both men are still on their feet.

The bigger man’s grin disappears. He lowers the knife, then sheathes it once there is space between you. “Keep your bloody rope.” His companion steers him up the pier, leaving the hauling coil where it lies. Neither touches the Kittiwake again.

Beside his brazier, Tammo has risen, one hand braced on his stool. He watches them go before looking toward the mooring. “Want me to check that line?”

### Narrator B *(roll proposed: intimidation DC 12)*

The thick-shouldered man stumbles backward with a startled grunt to avoid your lunge, his rusted knife jerking away from the mooring line. He doesn't fall. He catches his footing on the slick planks, his chest heaving as the tip of your longsword stays leveled at his throat. 

You hold the heavy steel steady between the two of them, demanding the pale man take his friend and walk away.

### Narrator C

The taller man catches his companion by the sleeve. “You heard her. Come away.”

The grin disappears from the barrel-chested man’s face. Neither man has fallen; your blade keeps him beyond reach of the mooring cleat, and he lowers the knife rather than test it. “All right. We’re going.” He folds it shut, then lets the other man pull him back toward the quay. They take their roller with them.

The line still holds the Kittiwake alongside, though a pale nick shows where the knife pressed into its tarred fibres. Tammo watches them leave before looking toward you.

“Want me to check that rope?”

## Turn 9 — aftermath

> **Player:** When they are gone I sit down on a crate with my hands shaking and look at what is left of the morning.

### Narrator A

There is still morning left: the herring wait in their wet crates, the Kittiwake rides beside the pier, and the hauling cradle stands empty farther up the shore. A gull lands near a loose fish, then hops back when Tammo scuffs his boot. The mooring line holds. No blood stains the boards.

Tammo brings his low seat over rather than asking you to move. He sits a little way off, leaving your shaking hands unremarked. For a while he watches the boat tug gently against her rope.

“I can count these with you,” he says. “Or I can sit here and keep the gulls off.” He rests his crooked fingers on his knees. “Which would help?”

### Narrator B *(fight started)*

You lower the point of your sword and step back toward the dripping crates, your hands shaking just as you intend to sit, fully expecting the two thugs to take the warning and walk away. 

They don't. 

The lean man’s blackened gums show as he grins, tapping the rusted iron boat hook against the wet wood once, twice. "She thinks we're taking orders, Corl," he snickers, stepping out to the left to cut off your path back up the quay. 

The thick-shouldered man, Corl, spits another dark wad of tobacco into the harbor. He steps right back up to the mooring line, raising his jagged, rusted knife. "Last chance to hand over the *Kittiwake*, little bird," he rumbles, closing the distance. "Or we take it off your corpse." 

They are fanning out, boxing you in against the freezing water. What do you do?

### Narrator C

The morning has narrowed to a stack of herring crates, a boat still afloat, and an account still unsettled. Gulls hop along the wet planks where spilled scales cling between the boards. Up the quay, a porter rolls an empty barrow toward the catch.

Tammo checks the nicked mooring line, then turns the damaged section outward where it can be watched. He brings the brazier closer to your crate without commenting on your shaking hands.

“Rope’ll hold for the hauling,” he says. “I’ll stay here with her.” He settles onto his stool and picks up the unfinished lashing. “You want company when the catch is counted?”

## Turn 10 — eerie

> **Player:** I row out alone toward Gannet Rock as the light goes, and I look up at the dark lighthouse.

### Narrator A

Saltmere’s windows shrink to yellow pinpricks behind the stern. Tammo’s parting directions carry across the sheltered water: “East by the Bell Channel! Half an hour in this water. Landing steps are on the harbor side!”

Beyond the breakwater, the swell lifts the oars between strokes. Gannet Rock’s lighthouse stands black against the last pale strip of sky; no lamp shines through its lantern windows. Water laps inside an iron ladder bolted to the rock beside the landing steps, each hollow rung giving a different note.

The steps are visible now, green with weed below the tide mark. A rusted mooring ring hangs above them. The swell leaves a brief, quieter interval between each lift of the boat. How do you approach the landing?

### Narrator B *(fight started)*

You turn toward the *Kittiwake*, determined to leave the thugs behind and cast off for Gannet Rock. But as you step toward the gunwale, the lean man's heavy iron boat hook snaps out, catching the links of your chain mail and yanking you violently back from the edge of the pier.

"Where do you think you're going?" he snarls, his rotted gums bared as he hauls you back onto the slick, wet wood. 

Corl doesn't hesitate. "Told you, little bird," the thick-shouldered man grunts, lunging forward with his rusted knife aimed straight for your ribs. There is no rowing away—they are blocking the boat, and they are going for blood.

### Narrator C

Saltmere’s shore lights shrink behind the stern; the lighthouse gives nothing back. Its tower stands black against the last strip of evening, with a few gulls circling the pale rock below it. Each pull of the oars leaves a brief wrinkle of reflected sky.

The painted board beside the harbor steps gave the passage plainly: **“Gannet Rock—east by the breakwater, half an hour at the oars.”** Beyond the sheltering wall, the swell lifts the little boat more slowly and heavily. Ahead, a whitewashed landing marker remains just visible beneath the tower; the rock’s western steps offer somewhere to put ashore.

Will you make for those steps?
